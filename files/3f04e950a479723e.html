The short answer: keep grease, coffee grounds, pasta, rice, potato peels, eggshells, fibrous vegetables like celery and onion skins, fruit pits, bones, and anything that is not food out of your garbage disposal. Soft food scraps in small amounts, rinsed down with cold water, are what the unit is actually built for.

We get called out for a lot of garbage disposal problems, and the honest truth is that most of them come down to what went into it. Disposals are built to handle small amounts of soft food scraps. They are not built to be a second trash can.

Here is the list we give homeowners in Mesa and the East Valley, along with what actually happens when these things go down the drain.

What should you never put down a garbage disposal?

Grease, oil and bacon fat. This is the single most common cause of the kitchen drain clogs we clear. Grease goes down warm and liquid, then cools and hardens inside the pipe, coating the line and catching everything that follows. Pour it into a can or jar and throw it out once it solidifies. We covered the full picture in how to prevent clogged drains.

Coffee grounds. They do not break down. They settle in the trap and build into a sludge that slows the whole kitchen line. Compost them or bin them.

Pasta, rice and bread. These keep absorbing water after they go down, swell, and turn into a paste that sticks to the inside of the pipe. A few strands rinsed off a plate are fine. A pot of leftover rice is not.

Eggshells. Opinions vary on this one, but we have pulled enough shell membrane out of disposals to keep it on the list. The thin membrane wraps around the grinding components, and the ground shell adds grit to any grease already sitting in the line.

Fibrous vegetables. Celery, corn husks, asparagus, onion skins, artichokes. The fibers wrap around the impellers and jam the unit. This is the most common reason a disposal hums but will not spin.

Potato peels. Same problem as rice. They break down into a starchy paste that coats everything downstream.

Fruit pits and bones. Disposals are not designed to grind hard objects. Pits and bones either jam the unit outright or bounce around and damage the grinding ring.

Anything that is not food. Twist ties, produce stickers, bottle caps, rubber bands, and the occasional spoon. We have seen all of them.

What can you safely put down a garbage disposal?

Soft fruit and vegetable scraps in small amounts, cooked meat scraps without bone, and small leftovers rinsed off a plate.

Run cold water before, during, and for about 15 seconds after grinding. Cold water keeps any grease solid so it travels through the line instead of coating it. Warm water does the opposite.

Feed scraps in gradually rather than packing the chamber full and switching it on.

Why is my garbage disposal humming but not spinning?

It is jammed, and the motor is trying to turn a blocked impeller.

  1. Turn the power off at the wall switch, and at the breaker if you can.
  2. Never put your hand into the unit, even with the power off.
  3. Find the hex slot on the bottom of the disposal and fit an Allen wrench into it.
  4. Work the wrench back and forth until the mechanism turns freely.
  5. Press the red reset button on the bottom of the unit.
  6. Restore power and try it again with cold water running.

If it still will not spin, or it hums and then cuts out, the motor may be seized. That is the point to call us for garbage disposal repair in Mesa.

That is a drain clog, not a disposal problem. The disposal is doing its job and the blockage is further down, usually in the trap or the line behind the wall.

Should you repair or replace a garbage disposal?

Replace it if any of these are true:

  • It is leaking from the bottom of the housing, which means the internal seal has failed
  • It trips the breaker every time you run it
  • It is more than 10 years old
  • The grinding chamber is rusted through

Repair is usually worth it for a jam, a failed reset switch, a leaking drain connection, or a bad splash guard.

Frequently asked questions

Are eggshells really bad for a garbage disposal?

The shell itself grinds down, but the thin membrane inside does not. It wraps around the grinding components and collects other debris. Combined with any grease already in the line, ground shell adds grit that helps build a clog, so we keep eggshells on the do-not-grind list.

Should I use hot or cold water with my garbage disposal?

Cold water, every time. Cold water keeps fats and grease solid so they move through the drain line as particles. Hot water melts grease, which then flows down the pipe, cools, and hardens on the pipe wall where it starts collecting everything else.

Can I put lemon peels down the disposal to clean it?

Small citrus peels are generally fine in a disposal in good condition, and they do help with odor. They will not clean the drain line itself. If the smell keeps coming back after grinding citrus, the odor is coming from buildup further down the drain, not from the disposal chamber.

Why does my garbage disposal smell?

Odor almost always comes from food residue stuck in the splash guard or coating the drain line. Lift and scrub the underside of the rubber splash guard first, since that is the most commonly missed spot. If the smell persists, buildup in the line is the likely cause.

How long should a garbage disposal last?

Most residential units last around 8 to 12 years. Builder-grade disposals installed in new construction tend to land at the lower end of that, and a unit that has been fed grease and fibrous scraps for years will fail sooner than one that has not.
