Summer is the busiest season for home drains. You cook and entertain more, the kids are home and using bathrooms all day, and guests come and go. Add sunscreen, pool sand, and yard debris to the mix, and it is easy to see why drains clog more between June and August. The good news is that knowing how to prevent clogged drains takes only a few habits, and most of them cost nothing. Below are practical, drain-by-drain tips you can start using today across Chandler, Gilbert, Mesa, and the rest of the East Valley.

Why do drains clog more in the summer?

Drains clog more in summer because home activity spikes and more debris hits your pipes. Heavier cooking sends grease and food scraps down the kitchen sink, while kids and guests mean more showers, more hair, and more flushing. In the Phoenix metro, pool season adds its own load. Sunscreen, sand, dirt, and grass clippings wash into shower and bathroom drains after every swim and every afternoon outdoors.

None of this is a problem on its own. The trouble starts when buildup collects week after week with no prevention in place. A drain that drains a little slower each day is quietly heading toward a full clog, often at the worst possible moment, like the morning before a backyard party.

How to prevent clogged drains in the kitchen

The best way to prevent clogged drains in the kitchen is to keep grease, oil, and fibrous food out of the sink entirely. Fats and oils look harmless as liquids, but they cool and harden inside your pipes, then trap everything that comes after them. According to Roto-Rooter, grease is one of the most common causes of kitchen drain clogs.

Use these kitchen habits all summer:

  • Never pour grease, fats, or cooking oil down the drain. Let it cool, then scrape it into the trash or a jar.
  • Skip the garbage disposal for fibrous foods. Celery, corn husks, onion skins, and potato peels wrap around blades and stick in pipes.
  • Keep coffee grounds and eggshells out of the sink. They clump together and settle like sediment.
  • Use a sink strainer to catch food scraps before they go down.
  • Run cold water while the disposal runs, and let it run a few seconds after.

These small steps matter most during cookouts and holiday weekends, when the kitchen sink works overtime.

How to keep bathroom drains clear

The fastest way to keep bathroom drains clear is to stop hair and "flushable" wipes from ever entering the pipes. Hair is the number one cause of slow shower and tub drains, and in Arizona summers that gets worse with all the rinsing of sunscreen, sand, and chlorine after pool days.

Protect every bathroom drain with these steps:

  • Add a hair catcher to each shower and tub drain, and empty it regularly.
  • Rinse sand and sunscreen off outside with a hose before pool gear or sandy kids hit the shower.
  • Brush hair before showering to cut down on loose strands.
  • Flush only toilet paper. Wipes, cotton products, and hygiene items belong in the trash.

That last point matters more than most people realize. Despite the label, wipes marketed as flushable do not break down like toilet paper. Wastewater agencies report that these wipes stay intact in the sewer, snag on pipe walls, and combine with grease to form stubborn blockages, as the Albemarle County Service Authority explains. When in doubt, throw it out.

Do not forget outdoor and yard drains

Outdoor and yard drains clog from the same summer debris that fills your gutters. Grass clippings, mulch, leaves, dirt, and garden runoff all wash toward patio drains, area drains, and downspout connections during monsoon storms and irrigation.

Keep them flowing with a few quick checks:

  • Clear leaves, clippings, and mulch off drain grates after mowing or yard work.
  • Point sprinklers away from open drains so they do not carry soil and debris into the line.
  • Watch for standing water near outdoor drains after watering, which can signal a buildup underground.

Yard drains are easy to ignore until they back up, so a five-minute pass after yard work goes a long way.

Are chemical drain cleaners bad for your pipes?

Chemical drain cleaners can be bad for your pipes, especially older or plastic plumbing. The harsh ingredients generate heat and corrosive reactions that can weaken pipe walls over time, and repeated use raises the risk of leaks and damage. Reliable Heating & Air notes that chemical drain cleaners can damage pipes and often only clear the surface of a clog while the real blockage remains.

There is another catch. Chemical cleaners tend to work only on minor clogs near the drain opening. If a clog keeps coming back, the chemical is masking a deeper problem, not solving it. A plunger, a drain snake, or a call to a plumber is safer for your plumbing and more effective in the long run.

When is a clog actually a sewer-line problem?

A single slow drain is usually local, but several drains backing up at once points to your main sewer line. That is the signal to stop the DIY fixes and call a professional.

Watch for these warning signs that a clog is becoming a bigger sewer issue:

  • Multiple drains run slow or back up at the same time (for example, a tub, toilet, and sink together).
  • Water rises in one fixture when you use another, such as the shower filling when you flush.
  • Gurgling sounds come from a toilet or drain when water runs elsewhere.
  • Foul sewer odors drift from drains or the yard.

These symptoms suggest the blockage sits in the main line, where store-bought products cannot reach. At that point you want professional drain cleaning in Chandler, and possibly sewer line repair to find the root cause with a camera. Recurring backups can also point to other hidden plumbing issues, so it helps to know the signs of a hidden water leak too.

FAQ: Preventing clogged drains this summer

What causes clogged drains most often?

The most common causes are grease and oil in the kitchen, hair in the bathroom, and wipes flushed down the toilet. In summer, sunscreen, pool sand, and yard debris add to the load. Most clogs build up slowly from a mix of these materials sticking to pipe walls.

How can I keep my drains clear naturally?

Flush drains with hot water regularly and use strainers and hair catchers to stop debris at the surface. A baking soda and vinegar rinse can help break up light buildup without harsh chemicals. The biggest win is prevention: keep grease, fibrous food, and wipes out of your pipes in the first place.

Are flushable wipes really safe to flush?

No. Wipes labeled flushable do not dissolve like toilet paper and are a leading cause of sewer clogs. They stay intact, snag inside pipes, and combine with grease to form blockages. The safest choice is to throw all wipes in the trash, even the ones marked flushable.

Should I use a chemical drain cleaner on a clog?

It is best to avoid them. Chemical drain cleaners can corrode and weaken pipes, especially older or plastic plumbing, and they often clear only the surface of a clog. A plunger or drain snake is safer, and a repeat clog usually needs a plumber rather than another bottle of cleaner.

When should I call a plumber for a clogged drain?

Call a plumber when more than one drain backs up at once, water rises in one fixture as you use another, or you hear gurgling and smell sewer odors. These point to a main-line clog that DIY methods cannot reach. A professional can clear it and inspect the line before it causes a backup.
