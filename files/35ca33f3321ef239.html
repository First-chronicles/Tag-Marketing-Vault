1. Executive summary

Overall verdict: these are not doorway pages, but they are not yet trusted local pages either. The system was rebuilt in July to September and it shows. Each page has its own intro, local history, named neighborhoods and its own FAQ set. The closest pair on the site (El Mirage and Youngtown) shares only 21.5% of its text. Google can reach every page: all 37 are indexable, self-canonical, in the sitemap, linked from the header menu everywhere, and identical on desktop and Googlebot mobile (one H1, same phone links, FAQ and Service schema present).

The main weaknesses are trust and proof, not duplication.

  • Local claims are unsourced. Pages state things like "Historic Downtown Glendale runs clay and cast iron laterals" or "Sun City West homes run cast iron drain lines". None of them cite a source, and two pages contradict themselves (Sun City West and Sun Lakes describe cast iron and clay on homes built in the plastic-pipe era).
  • The page tells people things Scott has ruled out. 24/7 or "around the clock" on 9 pages (including the Florence meta description), "same day" on 14, and a 24-hours-a-day opening schedule in the schema of all 37. Commercial work is promised on 7 pages, and repiping is in titles or H1s on 6.
  • No real proof of work. No local job examples, no visible reviews (only a 5-star badge image and a review link), and 4 stock images repeated on 34 to 35 pages. Each page has exactly one image of its own.
  • 16 pages answer "what warranty?" vaguely ("strong, long-term warranties") when the verified answer (20-year drain lining, 10-year ePIPE) is already approved.
  • Weak contextual linking. 9 pages, including Florence, Goodyear and Maricopa (three of the best-ranked), get no links from inside other pages' body copy. They are reached only through the menu.

Strengths: discoverability is solved; titles and metas are unique and city-aligned on all 37; FAQ schema is on every page; and several pages already have the kind of genuinely local content Google rewards. Examples: Fort McDowell (tribal jurisdiction and septic), Carefree (Liberty Utilities, Black Mountain Sewer), San Tan Valley (land subsidence) and the correct county permitting on unincorporated areas.

Largest opportunity: about 13 pages already have searches sitting at positions 8 to 20 (Buckeye, Florence, Avondale, Goodyear, Maricopa, Fort McDowell, Sun City and others). Pages are ranking but earning nothing, so the work is precision: answer the exact query, back the local facts with an official source, and add 2 or 3 neighbor links. The most useful verified local fact we found is who pays for which part of the sewer line in each city. It differs city by city: in Florence the owner pays even for the part under the street, while in Glendale and Avondale the city covers street-side repairs. Every homeowner with a broken line asks that question, and no competitor page in this market answers it per city.

Timing matters. Almost every city page was edited between Sep 15 and Oct 1 and is in a measurement window (first reads Oct 9 to late October). Re-editing a page before its read would erase the test. The plan in section 8 starts with work that does not touch those pages.

3. Templated and duplicate content

Method: every page's visible body text was cut into 8-word strings, with all city names and ZIP codes replaced by a placeholder so that "name swapped" text still counts as shared. Unique % = share of a page's strings that appear on no other city page.

  • Strong local differentiation (80%+): 11 pages, led by Buckeye 96%, Phoenix 91%, Carefree 91%, Sun City 89%, Mesa 88%.
  • Moderate template use (65 to 80%): 17 pages.
  • Heavy template use (49 to 65%): 9 pages: El Mirage 49%, Youngtown 55%, Goodyear 55%, Peoria 59%, Glendale 59%, Gilbert 62%, Anthem 62%, Sun Lakes 62%, Apache Junction 64%.
  • Near-duplicate / doorway risk: none. The highest pair overlap on the site is El Mirage and Youngtown at 21.5%, then Glendale and Peoria at 19.0%, Goodyear and Gilbert at 16.5%. Real duplicate trouble starts far higher.

What actually repeats (counted across the 37 pages):

  • The four benefit boxes, "No Demolition Solution / Up To 30% Cost Savings / Eco-friendly Repairs / Minimal Interruption": 36 to 37 pages. The Eco-friendly box says "your existing clay, cast iron, or copper lines" even on pages for cities built after 1995 (Anthem, Maricopa, San Tan Valley, Laveen, Queen Creek, Surprise).
  • H2 "Looking for a Pipe Repair Company Serving {City} Arizona?": 24 pages. H2 "We Are {City}'s Pipelining Experts": 18. "Pipe Lining Advantages": 29.
  • FAQ questions: "What warranties do you offer in {City}?" 22 pages; "How do you diagnose the problem before starting?" 20; "Do I need a permit for sewer or water line work in {City}?" 16; "How long does a trenchless job take?" 15. 350 FAQ questions in total; the repeated ones are the generic half.
  • "{City} homeowners typically save up to 30% versus dig-and-replace..." on 22 pages.
  • Images: 4 stock images on 34 to 35 pages; one image of its own per page; Mesa and Paradise Valley have none of their own.

The SEO risk in plain terms: not a penalty. The risk is that Google sees the shared shell as the "main content" on the shorter pages and treats them as interchangeable. That is a ranking ceiling, not a demotion. The fix is to replace the shared blocks on the 9 heavy pages with real local substance, not to paraphrase them 37 ways.
