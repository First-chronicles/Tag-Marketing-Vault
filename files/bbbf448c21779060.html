Added at the topNew question heading and quick answer:What causes pinhole leaks in copper pipes? Pinhole leaks in copper pipes are caused by corrosion working from the inside of the pipe, usually driven by water chemistry such as low pH (acidic) water or high levels of chlorides. Over years the copper wall thins until tiny holes open. A single pinhole is often a sign that the same corrosion is at work elsewhere in the system, so the fix is either replacing the affected pipe or restoring the whole system from the inside with an epoxy coating such as ePIPE.

FAQ added (4 questions)(none on the page)
  1. Can an epoxy coating stop pinhole leaks in copper pipes?
    Yes, when the pipe is still sound enough to coat. The ePIPE process dries the pipes with heated air, cleans them with an air and sand mixture, and coats the inside with epoxy, which seals pinholes and protects against new corrosion. The coating is NSF/ANSI 61 certified for drinking water.
  2. How do you find a pinhole leak you cannot see?
    A water meter that keeps moving with every tap and appliance off is a common first sign. Pipeliners locates hidden leaks with pressure testing, acoustic listening and thermal imaging before anything is opened. Learn about leak detection in Phoenix.
  3. Should I repipe or coat my copper pipes after a pinhole leak?
    It depends on how far the corrosion has spread. Repeat pinhole leaks across the house point to restoring or replacing the whole system rather than patching one spot. Our guide to repiping vs relining compares the two.
  4. Is one pinhole leak a sign of more to come?
    Often, yes. Pinholes form where corrosion has thinned the pipe wall, and the same water runs through the rest of the copper, so other sections are usually wearing too. An inspection shows how far it has spread.

FAQ added (4 questions)(none on the page)
  1. Can a sewer line leak be fixed without digging?
    Often, yes. If the camera shows the pipe still holds its shape, a cured-in-place liner can seal cracks and leaking joints from the inside. A collapsed section usually has to be dug up and replaced first. See how to repair a sewer line without digging.
  2. How do you find where a sewer line is leaking?
    A sewer camera inspection shows the inside of the line and exactly where cracks, separated joints and roots are, so only the problem area is worked on.
  3. What causes a sewer line to leak?
    The most common causes are tree roots growing into joints, cracked or corroded clay and cast iron pipe, and joints that shift as the soil settles.
  4. Is a leaking sewer line an emergency?
    A slow leak is not usually an emergency, but it gets worse over time and can wash out the soil under slabs and walkways. Sewage backing up into the house needs attention right away.

FAQ added (4 questions)(none on the page)
  1. What pipe repairs can a homeowner do themselves?
    Small, reachable jobs such as tightening a loose fitting, replacing a worn washer or clearing a simple sink clog with a plunger. Anything buried, inside a wall or under the slab needs a proper diagnosis first.
  2. Why should I avoid chemical drain cleaners?
    Harsh chemicals are hard on old metal pipe and do not remove hard scale, so the clog tends to come back. For blockages, Pipeliners recommends hydro jetting instead.
  3. Can I line or coat my own pipes?
    No. Lining a drain or coating water lines takes a camera inspection, cleaning equipment, the liner or epoxy system and curing equipment, plus the training to install them correctly.
  4. How do I know if a DIY fix did not work?
    Clogs that return, a leak that reappears in the same spot, damp walls or floors, or a water meter that keeps moving with everything off. Any of these means it is time for a camera inspection or leak detection.
