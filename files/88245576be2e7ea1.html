What 2026 search rewards

Three surfaces, one business identity

All three surfaces read the same pages and the same outside listings. What differs is what each one takes from them: Google ranks pages, answer engines quote short passages, and AI chat tools recommend businesses they see confirmed across many sources.

SEO

Google blue links and the map pack

Where the calls come from today
  • Map pack ranking runs on proximity, reviews, GBP categories and consistent NAP.
  • Deep, specific service pages beat thin city-swap pages. Google's scaled-content and doorway rules target copies with only the city name changed.
  • One clean business entity in schema, matching GBP exactly.

Summit today: technical base is solid (93). The hubs are thin, 18 of 121 city pages are near-copies, and the schema describes the business three different ways.

AEO

AI Overviews, snippets, voice

Google quotes a passage instead of sending the click
  • A 40 to 60 word direct answer right under the H1.
  • H2s written as the questions homeowners ask, each answered on its own without "see our other page".
  • Service and FAQ schema that matches the visible page.

Summit today: the blog posts already follow this pattern. Service and city pages open with three boilerplate H2s and have 0 to 1 question headings.

GEO

ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot, AI Mode

AI tools recommend businesses they can confirm elsewhere
  • Brands get cited through third-party sources far more often than through their own site: reviews, BBB, Yelp, Angi, YouTube, Reddit, local news.
  • ChatGPT and Copilot lean on Bing, so Bing Webmaster Tools and Bing Places matter.
  • AI crawlers need access, server-rendered content, and pages refreshed about once a quarter.

Summit today: content is server-rendered and robots.txt allows every AI bot. Authority is 9/100, and SiteGround bot protection may be serving AI crawlers a CAPTCHA page.

What the crawl found

Problems ranked by impact

Critical

The business's identity data contradicts itself

  • The About page (ID 4222) has a hand-coded schema block with Utah coordinates (40.23, -111.66). It also links a logo that returns 404, points to 8 old URLs that redirect, and leaves the license number empty.
  • Three different hours: the site text says Mon to Sat 8 to 5, Rank Math says Mon to Sun 9 to 5, and the About block says Mon to Fri 7 to 6 plus Sat 8 to 2.
  • Rank Math's main business entity has no address, phone, map coordinates or social/GBP links.
  • City pages output a second, unlinked Plumber entity from the location mu-plugin.

Why it matters: Google and the AI tools merge these signals into one "who is this business" record. Conflicting hours and a Utah pin lower their trust in every other claim.

Critical, unverified

AI crawlers may be getting a CAPTCHA page

robots.txt allows every AI bot, and test requests with GPTBot, PerplexityBot and ClaudeBot user agents got full pages. But SiteGround already serves Kim's IP a CAPTCHA stub marked noindex. If it does the same to real AI crawler IPs, those tools see nothing. Only the SiteGround bot-protection logs can confirm it either way.

High

Service and city pages are not built to be quoted

  • Every service and city page opens with "Serving the Phoenix Valley", "Schedule a Free Estimate Today!" and "Request Your Free Estimate" as H2s. Some also use H2s for "Call Us Today!", the phone number, and "we serve".
  • There's no direct answer under the H1. The hub intros talk about "Phoenix Valley" instead of the service.
  • Mesa FAQ answer "Yes. See the emergency plumber in Mesa page." can't be quoted by an AI tool.
High

Hubs are thinner than their own city pages, and some cities are copies

  • /water-heaters/ has 672 words, mostly generic. The /slab-leaks/ H1 is just "Slab Leaks".
  • The Gilbert and Chandler drain cleaning pages are 70% identical, including a 483-word FAQ with only the city swapped. The water heater city pages are well differentiated and are the model to follow.
  • Every page is marked as an Article in schema. The hubs have no Service schema.
High

Unverified claims and a placeholder are live

  • /about/ says "licensed and insured in the state of Arizona (Lic. #)". The number is missing.
  • "Work Guaranteed" (/about/), "Customer Satisfaction Guaranteed" (sewer page) and "we can guarantee..." (/water-heaters/) are not on the approved-claims list.
  • The sitewide "Same Day Service" badge has no "most calls" qualifier. The "4.8 · 516+ Google Reviews" badge needs checking against GBP.
Medium

Blog trust signals and housekeeping

  • Blog author is the brand name typed as a Person, and the author page is noindex. No named licensed plumber appears anywhere, and posts show no "Updated" date. /blog/ has no H1.
  • No /llms.txt. Four Google Fonts files load, including unused Roboto and Roboto Slab, and jQuery blocks rendering.
  • Nearly every sitemap URL has the same Sep 9 or 10 lastmod date. /terms-of-use/ and /terms-of-service/ are both listed. /pricing/ is noindex but still live, with Lorem ipsum on it.
  • Homepage, report only: two H1s, no H2s, and the H1 says "Phoenix Valley" while the target is "plumber mesa az". This waits on Scott.

Already fine: all content is server-rendered (AI crawlers can read it without JavaScript), canonicals and robots tags are correct, NAP and ROC #338366 are in every footer, and FAQ schema matches the visible FAQs. No OTTO script, no 24/7 claims, no competitor names.
