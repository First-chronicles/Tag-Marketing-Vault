Why do commercial sewer line problems spike during monsoon Arizona storms?

Commercial sewer line problems during monsoon Arizona storms spike because intense rainfall hits a drainage system that was not built for that volume all at once. Three things stack up fast.

  • Groundwater infiltration: Rain saturates the soil and forces water into any crack, joint gap, or root intrusion in the pipe, filling lines that should be carrying waste away.
  • Debris and grease washout: Storm runoff pushes silt, gravel, leaves, and grease deeper into commercial drains, where it collects at low points and forms blockages.
  • Surge loading: When the municipal system backs up under storm volume, that pressure can travel back toward your building and into your lowest fixtures.

For a restaurant, retail store, or office building, that combination means the problem rarely stays small. It shows up as a flooded floor drain during the lunch rush or a backed-up restroom on a busy Saturday.

What are the warning signs of a sewer problem at a commercial property?

The warning signs of a commercial sewer problem are slow drains, gurgling, odor, and water showing up where it should not. Catching them early is the difference between a quick service call and a closure. Watch for these around your building.

  • Multiple drains running slow at the same time, not just one fixture
  • Gurgling sounds from toilets or floor drains when other water runs
  • Sewage odor near restrooms, kitchens, or exterior cleanouts
  • Water pooling near floor drains, in back-of-house areas, or in the parking lot
  • Backups that get worse during or right after a storm

If more than one of these shows up after a monsoon downpour, the issue is usually in the main line, not a single fixture. That is the point to call a pro before it spreads.
