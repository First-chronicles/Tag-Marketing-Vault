Pipeliners Content Plan

Eight new guides and a steady refresh cycle over the next 12 weeks, built around the three services Pipeliners actually sells: lining drain and sewer pipes, ePIPE water line restoration, and hydro jetting. Every topic has measured search demand (100+ searches a month, difficulty 30 or lower) and no existing page already covers it.

Prepared Oct 1, 2026. Sources: Google Search Console (Jul 2 to Sep 28, 2026), saved Search Atlas keyword research (US monthly volume and difficulty), and a crawl of all 106 live pages on Sep 30.

Where things stand

The blog gets seen, not clicked

The 51 blog posts earned about zero clicks in the last 90 days. They show up for real searches, but mostly on pages 5 to 10 of Google, where nobody clicks.

Demand exists close to the services

Google already shows the site for cipp installation (1,217 impressions, position 63), cured in place pipe lining (869, position 84) and what causes pinhole leaks in copper plumbing (693, position 44). One query already ranks near the top: pipe relining vs replacement cost at position 2.

Some demand cannot be served

The site also appears for whole house repipe (310 impressions) and for gas line and manhole terms. Scott confirmed on Oct 1 that Pipeliners does not do full repipes or manhole lining, so this plan writes nothing for that demand.

Rules every piece follows

Only confirmed claimsWarranty 20 years on drain lining, 10 years on ePIPE. Up to 30% savings. Liners 50+ years, ePIPE 75+. Slab leaks fixed by lining the pipe. Every job starts with a camera inspection.
Never write24/7, same-day, prices, full repiping, manhole lining, pipe bursting, gas lines. Scott, Oct 1.
Answer firstThe first paragraph answers the search. Headings are phrased as the questions people type. Each post ends with a short FAQ and a "last updated" date.
House style"Pipeliners" in new copy, no em dashes, no competitor names, outside links only to .gov, .edu or standards bodies. "Relining" or "lining the pipe" instead of the noun "pipe lining".
Ad pages stay untouchedNew posts can link to pages that run ads, but those pages are only edited with a specific OK.
Named authorEach post carries a real byline (Jake Shaker or Scott Vincent) so Google and AI tools can tie it to a person with trade experience.

Who the content is for

Homeowners with a problem now

Phoenix Valley owners of 1950s to 1990s homes with a backup, a smell, a slab leak or repeat clogs. They search the symptom first, then "how is it fixed", then a local company.

Buyers and sellers

People in the middle of a home sale who hear "get a sewer scope". High value: they need an inspection this week and often a repair quote.

Property and business owners

Restaurants, offices, rentals and HOAs where downtime costs money. Smaller search volume, bigger jobs.

How the posts link together

  • Every new post links up to its cluster hub, and the hub links down to every post in the cluster.
  • Every post links to the one service page it leads to: drain and sewer relining, ePIPE, or hydro jetting. 16 of the 51 current posts link to no service page today; the Nov 16 link pass fixes that.
  • Posts cross-link to 2 or 3 related posts in the same cluster, using words already in the sentence as the link.
  • Local posts (Orangeburg, sewer scope before you buy) link to 1 or 2 city pages where the detail genuinely applies. No lists of city links.
  • New posts may link to pages that run ads. Those pages are never edited to link back without a specific OK.

What makes these posts worth citing

Pipeliners' own job data

The best material only Pipeliners has: what the camera actually finds in Valley sewer lines (roots, bellies, Orangeburg, cast iron scale), counted from real jobs. A few numbers from Scott or Jake, even rough ones, would make these posts the only source with them.

Real job photos and footage stills

Before and after camera stills from actual jobs, credited to Pipeliners. AI tools and Google both favor pages showing work nobody else can show.

Local public sources

City water hardness reports, building-era records and utility rules, cited from .gov sources only. This detail separates a Phoenix guide from a national one.

Sharing beyond the website

The sewer scope and cleanout guides are useful to realtors, home inspectors and HOA boards. Sharing them there, which you or Scott would do, earns mentions that the website alone cannot.
