What are the most common signs of a hidden water leak?

The most common signs of a hidden water leak are an unexplained spike in your water bill, the sound of water running when no fixtures are on, and a musty or moldy smell. These three show up before any visible water does, which is what makes them worth knowing.

Watch for these signs:

  • An unexplained high water bill. If your usage climbs without a clear reason, water is going somewhere. Compare this month to the same month last year, not just last month, since summer irrigation throws off month to month numbers.
  • The sound of running or trickling water when every faucet, toilet, and appliance is off.
  • A persistent musty or earthy smell, which often means moisture is trapped behind drywall or under flooring.
  • Warm spots on the floor, a classic sign of a hot water line leaking under a concrete slab.
  • Stains, bubbling paint, or sagging on ceilings and walls.
  • A drop in water pressure across the whole house.
  • New cracks in the foundation or floor, which can follow long term moisture under the slab.
  • A lush, extra green patch in the yard over a buried supply or sewer line.

Any one of these is worth a closer look. Two or more together is a strong reason to call a plumber.

How do I check for a hidden leak with the water meter test?

Use the water meter test: turn off all water in the home, note the meter reading, wait two hours without using any water, then check the reading again. If the meter moved at all, you likely have a leak somewhere on your side of the line. The EPA recommends this exact check during its annual Fix a Leak Week campaign.

Here is the step by step:

  1. Turn off every faucet, appliance, and water using fixture inside and outside the house.
  2. Find your water meter and write down the reading, including the small leak indicator dial if yours has one.
  3. Wait two hours and use no water during that window.
  4. Read the meter again. Any change points to a hidden leak.

For toilets specifically, drop a few drops of food coloring in the tank and wait ten minutes. If color appears in the bowl without flushing, the flapper is leaking. A running toilet is one of the most common hidden water wasters in any home.

Why does a high water bill point to a hidden leak in summer?

A high water bill points to a hidden leak when your usage rises faster than your habits explain. Summer makes this tricky in Chandler, Gilbert, Mesa, and Tempe because landscape irrigation, pool top offs, and heavy cooling demand all push the bill up on their own. A leak hides inside that seasonal noise.

To tell a leak apart from normal summer use, compare your current bill to the same month last year rather than to last month. If this June is far higher than last June with no change in household size or landscaping, suspect a leak. Household leaks across the country waste close to a trillion gallons of water a year, and fixing them can save the average home about ten percent on its water bill, according to EPA WaterSense.

What does a warm spot on the floor mean?

A warm spot on the floor usually means a hot water line is leaking under your concrete slab. Heat from the escaping hot water rises through the slab and warms one section of tile or vinyl, so it feels noticeably warmer than the floor around it when you walk barefoot.

Slab leaks are serious because the water has nowhere to drain. It pools under the foundation, which can lead to cracks, uneven floors, and major structural repairs if left alone. A warm spot paired with a higher bill and the sound of running water is a near certain sign of a slab leak that needs a professional fast.

Can a hidden leak cause mold and water damage?

Yes. A hidden leak feeds mold and water damage because it keeps building materials wet day after day. Mold can begin growing on damp drywall, insulation, or framing within 24 to 48 hours of contact with water, according to the EPA's guide to mold and moisture. That is why a musty smell often shows up before any stain does. Even in a dry desert climate, the trapped moisture from a hidden leak stays sealed behind walls and under floors, so mold still takes hold.

Once moisture sits behind a wall, the damage compounds. Drywall softens, wood rots, paint bubbles, and mold spreads into cavities you cannot see. By the time water shows on the surface, the problem has often been building for weeks. Catching the signs early keeps a small plumbing repair from turning into a remediation and rebuild project.

How do professionals find a hidden leak without tearing up the house?

Professionals find hidden leaks with non invasive tools that pinpoint the source before any wall or floor is opened. This is the key advantage of calling a plumber instead of guessing and cutting into drywall.

Common methods include:

  • Acoustic listening devices that amplify the sound of water escaping a pressurized pipe.
  • Thermal imaging cameras that spot temperature differences from a hot or cold water leak behind a surface.
  • Pressure testing to confirm a line is losing water and narrow down which line.
  • Moisture meters that map where water has spread.

Frequently asked questions about hidden water leaks

How can I tell if a leak is hidden or just a dripping faucet?

A visible dripping faucet is easy to see and hear at the fixture. A hidden leak shows up indirectly, through a rising water bill, musty smells, warm or damp spots, stains, or the sound of running water when everything is off. If you can find no source for the symptoms, the leak is likely behind a wall, under a slab, or in a buried line.

Will a hidden water leak go away on its own?

No. A hidden water leak does not heal itself and almost always gets worse over time as pipes corrode or fittings loosen further. The longer it runs, the more water it wastes and the more damage it does to framing, flooring, and your foundation. Early repair is always cheaper than waiting.

Is a small hidden leak really worth fixing?

Yes. Even a slow hidden leak can waste thousands of gallons a year and feed mold within a day or two of soaking nearby materials. Small leaks also tend to grow. Fixing one early protects your home and trims your water bill, which is why the EPA encourages homeowners to find and fix leaks promptly.

Are hidden leaks more common during Arizona monsoon season?

Monsoon season, roughly mid June through September, brings heavy rain and humidity that can make existing leaks easier to notice and harder to trace. The added moisture can mask damp spots and feed mold faster, while peak summer water use hides a rising bill. If you suspect a leak during monsoon months, the water meter test still gives you a clear answer.

What should I do if I think I have a hidden leak right now?

Shut off your main water valve if you suspect active flooding, then run the water meter test to confirm the leak. After that, call a licensed plumber for professional detection rather than cutting into walls yourself. Pinpointing the source first keeps the repair small.
