How to Use Low-Hanging Fruit Keywords

A step-by-step picture guide: how to find keywords Google already shows us for, and how to move them from page 2 or 3 up to page 1, without guessing.

What "low-hanging fruit" means

A search Google already shows one of our pages for, again and again, just not high enough to get clicks. The page is close. A few small, focused fixes can push it up.

Numbers are Google's average position. Page 1 = positions 1 to 10, page 2 = 11 to 20, page 3 = 21 to 30.

The 6 steps

1 Confirm it's real
2 Match the right page
3 Put the keyword where Google reads first
4 Add what's missing
5 Link to it
6 Tell Google, then wait

You need

Search Console access (pipelinersusa.com Domain property), the page in WordPress, and 30 minutes per keyword.

House rules

No prices or unverified claims. "Pipeliners", not "Pipeliners USA". No em dashes. Never touch the homepage, phone numbers or Google Ads pages.

What this example tells us

"cipp pipe lining cost" is split: the CIPP vs Traditional post gets 19 impressions at position 63 (too far down), and What is CIPP gets 3 at position 3, but only on Aug 30 and 31. It's a real search, but not low-hanging fruit yet, and it's a cost topic, so the wording needs Scott's OK first.

Does the page really answer it?

  • A cost search needs a cost answer (what drives the price, no prices).
  • A Phoenix / Mesa search needs local detail, usually on the city page.
  • A "how to" / "what is" search needs a clear explanation, usually a blog post.
  • A "near me" / "company" search needs a service page with a clear call to action.

Stop if…

  • The only page ranking is the homepage or a Google Ads page. Those are off-limits.
  • The keyword names another company.
  • It's a service Pipeliners doesn't offer or Scott hasn't confirmed.
  • The page is on hold for a scheduled review.

4Add what the page is missing

Google moves a page up when it answers the search better than the pages above it. Add one short section or 1–2 FAQs, not a full rewrite.

Good additions

  • A short section that answers the exact question.
  • 1–2 FAQs people really ask (check the Queries tab for wording).
  • Arizona detail: caliche soil, clay and cast iron pipes, desert tree roots.
  • How the job works: camera inspection first, then the repair.

Never add

  • Prices, "free", warranty years, "only company that…" claims unless Scott confirms them.
  • Links to competitors or untrusted sites.
  • Services Pipeliners doesn't offer (for example gas lines).
  • Filler or AI-sounding phrases.

6Tell Google, then wait

Ask Google to re-read the page, then leave it alone for 4 weeks. Changing it again too soon makes it impossible to tell what worked.

How to compare fairly

  • Search Console → Date → Compare → two 28-day windows.
  • Same keyword filter, same page.
  • Look at position, impressions, then clicks.

What counts as a win

  • Average position moves up (lower number).
  • More impressions on more days.
  • First clicks from that keyword.
