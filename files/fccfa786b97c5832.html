The short version

  1. More people clicked through to the website from Google. 28 in June, 38 in September. That is a real improvement.
  2. Summit showed up in Google far less often. Most of that is fine. Those were searches where Summit was sitting on page 4 or 5, where nobody ever clicks.
  3. But a few important searches got worse. "plumber near me" and "emergency plumber tempe" both slipped, and three local searches disappeared completely.
  4. The home page slipped too, and that is the page that brings in about two thirds of all clicks.
  5. The biggest issue is not on this page at all. Almost half of Summit's clicks come from people typing the company name. People searching for a plumber, who do not know Summit yet, mostly are not finding it.

Why the website was rebuilt in the middle of this

The new site went live on September 3, right in the middle of the "after" period. 86 new pages went live the day before that.

That means September is a transition month, not a final score. A lot of the movement in this report is Google re-sorting a site that changed a great deal, all at once.

A quick note on what these words mean

Showed up means Summit appeared somewhere in Google's results, even on page 5 where nobody looks.

Clicked through means somebody actually chose Summit and visited the site.

Average spot is where Summit typically sits. Spot 27 means page 3. Roughly 9 in 10 people never go past page 1.

Fewer people seeing us sounds bad. Usually it is not.

Think of it like a phone book that listed Summit on page 40. Being removed from page 40 costs nothing, because nobody was turning to page 40.

That is most of what happened. Pages that were sitting at spot 40 to 50 moved up into the twenties, and shed all the pointless listings on the way. Queen Creek went from spot 49 to 26. San Tan Valley went from 43 to 25. The Mesa sewer page went from 40 to 18, the biggest jump on the whole site.

It dropped from spot 17 to spot 24 and lost 3 clicks. That sounds small, but the home page brings in about two thirds of every click Summit gets from Google. When it moves, everything moves.

Section 03

The main problem

People who already know Summit can find it. People looking for a plumber cannot.

Almost half of all clicks come from people typing the company name

55 out of 127 clicks came from searches containing the word "Summit". Those people already knew the business. They found it easily, at around spot 8.

Take those away and Summit earned roughly 72 clicks from 46,000 appearances in front of people who did not know the name. That is the real gap, and it is what any growth work has to close.

Half the visibility earns nothing

17 pages account for half of everywhere Summit appears in Google, and between them they produced 2 clicks.

10 of those went live on September 2 and are simply too new to judge. Give them until November. The other 7 have been live for months and are the real concern.

Some searches we can never win

There are other towns in America called Summit, and Google is matching them to us. Real searches Summit is appearing for include "water heater replacement mount summit" (that is Indiana) and "hall summit" (Louisiana).

We also appear for "costa mesa sewer line repair", which is California matching on the word Mesa. None of these can ever turn into a customer.

One page is answering the wrong question

The single biggest search Summit appears for, outside of the company name, is "emergency plumber tempe". It appeared 2,495 times and got zero clicks, sitting at spot 28.

The page Google is sending those people to is the general Tempe plumbing page, not an emergency page. Summit has emergency pages. They are not the ones showing up.

Section 04

What to fix first

In order. The first two are the cheapest wins available.

Quick wins

  1. Two pages are already close to page 1 and getting nothing. The Tempe water softener page sits between spot 9 and 14 for its main searches and has never been clicked. Slab leaks is the same. Something is sitting above them taking the click, most likely the map results. Worth an hour to find out.
  2. Give "emergency plumber tempe" a proper page. 2,495 appearances, zero clicks, currently answered by the wrong page.
  3. Work out why the home page slipped and report to Scott.

Bigger jobs

  1. Decide about the 5 pages stuck on page 3 to 6. Nothing we write in a title will pull a page from spot 42 to page 1. Either commit to properly ranking them or stop counting them as opportunity.
  2. Check what counts as a lead in Analytics. Summit's phone numbers rotate, so call tracking may be missing calls. Worth confirming before anyone reads anything into the lead numbers.
  3. Take a before picture every time from now on. We cannot prove what the September speed work achieved because no measurement was taken beforehand. That stops now.
Section 05

What we cannot say yet

Three honest limits. Worth reading before anyone draws a conclusion.

A week of September traffic went to the wrong account

The new website was built from a copy of Summit's Utah site, and the Google Analytics connection came with it. From the afternoon of September 3 until September 10, Arizona's visitor data was recorded in the Utah account instead.

The traffic is not lost, it is just filed in the wrong drawer. Scott reconnected it properly on September 12. But every September visitor number is about a week short, so no drop in visits should be treated as a real decline until that week is recovered.

We cannot yet prove the speed work helped

Images and scripts were optimised on September 16. No speed measurement was taken before that, and Google does not publish real visitor speed data for this site because it does not get enough traffic yet.

So the speed work may well have helped. We simply cannot show it. From the next change onward we will take a before reading every time.

September is a transition month

A brand new website, 86 new pages, and an analytics fault all landed inside the "after" period. That is far too much at once to call September a pass or a fail.

The fair read is: the direction is encouraging, the foundations moved, and the next clean comparison is October.

Website speed

Google has no real speed data for this site

Google only publishes real-visitor speed scores once a site gets enough traffic. Summit is below that line, so both Search Console and PageSpeed report "No data".

This means speed cannot help or hurt Summit's ranking right now, because Google has no speed data to judge it on. It still matters for visitors, which is why the test below is worth looking at.

The test result, home page on a phone

Score 38 out of 100. The main content takes 5 seconds to appear, where under 2.5 is considered good.

The biggest cause is 1,225 KB of unused code, not images. Images are only 175 KB of the possible saving, so more image compression will not move this much. The scripts are the work.
